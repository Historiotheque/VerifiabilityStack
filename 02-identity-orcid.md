# 02 — Identity: ORCID

*Last tested: 2026-09-21 (manual "Add DOI" flow, §3).*

## What this chapter gives you

An **ORCID iD**: a permanent numeric identifier that attaches your
works to exactly one person — you — regardless of name collisions,
monikers, or institutional affiliation. It is the identity layer of
the stack (see Ch. 01, §3): everything else you build will point back
to it.

## 0. What ORCID is (thirty seconds)

ORCID (orcid.org) is a nonprofit registry that issues researchers a
unique, lifelong identifier. It looks like this:

`0000-0000-0000-0000` (placeholder — yours will be a real number)

It solves the name-collision problem: search engines may confuse you
with three other people sharing your name, but nothing confuses two
different ORCID iDs. It is free, it is yours independent of any
employer or institution, and it is the single most widely recognized
researcher identifier in the world.

## 1. Register

1. Go to **orcid.org** and choose to register for a new iD.
2. Enter your name as **[YOUR FULL NAME]**. If you publish or exhibit
   under a different name or moniker — many artists do — do not try
   to squeeze it into this field. You will add it separately in §2.
3. Use a **permanent email address** — `[YOUR EMAIL]` — ideally one
   you control independent of any institution, employer, or ISP.
   This address is your recovery path for the next decades; choose
   accordingly.
4. Set the default visibility to **Everyone**. You are building a
   public record; a private default defeats the purpose. (Individual
   items can still be restricted later if needed.)
5. Confirm via the verification email ORCID sends you.

You now have an iD. The canonical form of the link is:

`https://orcid.org/[YOUR ORCID ID]`

## 2. Make the record findable

A bare iD with no metadata is barely better than no iD. Fill in:

- **Other names:** every name or moniker under which your work
  circulates. > **Example (fictional).** *Sam K., an independent
  painter-researcher, registers as "Samuel K." and adds "Sam K." and
  the moniker "SK" as other names, so works published under any of
  them resolve to one person.*
- **Country, keywords:** plain terms describing your practice. If you
  maintain a controlled vocabulary of tags (see Ch. 05 on
  announcement), the plain-language versions belong here.
- **Websites:** your site, your profiles — the addresses where the
  work actually lives.
- **Biography:** one or two sentences, public-facing. Who you are
  and what you do, nothing more.

Keep every field's visibility on **Everyone** unless you have a
specific reason not to.

## 3. Add a work manually via its DOI (recommended path)

This is the low-headache path, and the one this guide recommends as
your default. Every time you publish something that gets a DOI —
a Zenodo record in Ch. 04, for example — take one minute to link it
here by hand.

1. Sign in at orcid.org and open your record.
2. In the **Works** section, choose **Add**, then **Add DOI**.
   (Labels may shift slightly over time; you are looking for the
   option that adds a work by its DOI.)
3. Paste the DOI — e.g. `[YOUR DOI]` — and let ORCID fetch the
   metadata. **Check that the title is correct** before saving.
4. Confirm or set the work type to the closest match (e.g. *Image*
   for visual works) and set visibility to **Everyone**.
5. Save. The work now appears on your public record, permanently
   linked to its DOI.

One minute per work. It always works.

## 4. The alternative: automatic updates

ORCID also lets you authorize external services as **trusted
organizations**, so they can push works to your record automatically
instead of you adding them by hand.

Honest assessment: convenient when it works, fiddly when it doesn't
— authorizations lapse, interfaces change, and troubleshooting costs
more time than the minute the manual path takes. If the automatic
route fights you, abandon it without guilt and use §3. The manual
path is the guide's recommended default precisely because it has no
moving parts.

## 5. Put the iD to work

An identifier nobody sees is an identifier wasted:

- Add `https://orcid.org/[YOUR ORCID ID]` to your website, your CV,
  and your social profiles.
- Use it in every context where identity matters: submissions,
  applications, correspondence with editors and curators.
- It compounds: each linked work makes the record — and you —
  easier to find and harder to confuse with anyone else.

## Pitfalls

- **Visibility set to "Only me."** The work sits on your record but
  is invisible to the world. After adding anything, check that it
  is set to Everyone.
- **Duplicate records.** Registering twice with different emails
  splits your identity in two — the exact problem the iD exists to
  solve. ORCID can merge duplicates, but prevention (one permanent
  email, §1) is better.
- **Moniker in the wrong field.** Keep the primary name stable and
  put alternate names under "other names," or works published under
  the moniker won't connect cleanly.
- **Losing the registration email.** An address tied to a job, a
  school, or an ISP you might leave is a time bomb. Decades means
  decades.

## Checklist

- [ ] Registered with a permanent, institution-independent email
- [ ] Default visibility: Everyone
- [ ] Other names / monikers added
- [ ] Keywords, websites, one-sentence biography filled in
- [ ] First work added via DOI (§3), visibility Everyone
- [ ] iD link on website, CV, and profiles
